# Mentoring a Junior Developer Through React Fundamentals

## Purpose

Engineering story documenting hands-on mentoring at Advision Development. Use it for mentoring, coaching, and "tell me about helping someone grow" questions: it is the concrete example behind the general "mentored junior developers" claims.

Facts live here; resume bullets and interview answers reference this file, they do not duplicate it.

**Employer:** Advision Development, during the **Lead Web Developer** period (2020). See [`../experience/advision.md`](../experience/advision.md) for role context.

---

## Context

A recently graduated developer joined the team. She was still learning **React** and was struggling with the fundamentals: enough that completing her assigned tickets was a real challenge, not a matter of polish.

As the most experienced React developer on the team, helping her become productive was my responsibility, both practically (the team needed her output) and as part of how I understand seniority.

---

## What I Did

- **Sat with her regularly, most evenings**, walking through React fundamentals: how components, props, state, and rendering actually work, not just how to copy patterns from the codebase.
- **Taught the basics deliberately** rather than fixing her tickets for her. The goal was that she could reason about React herself, not that her current ticket got merged.
- **Kept it sustained.** This was consistent time investment over a stretch of time, not a one-off explanation session.

---

## Impact

She grasped the fundamentals well and **started completing her tickets independently**. The team gained a productive developer, and she gained a foundation she could build on rather than a dependency on me.

**No timeline or productivity metrics are documented. Do not invent them.**

---

## Lessons Learned

- **Consistent short sessions beat heroic one-off explanations.** Fundamentals stick through repetition and applied practice, not a single lecture.
- **Teach the model, not the ticket.** Fixing someone's code unblocks a sprint; teaching them how the framework thinks unblocks a career.
- **Mentoring is a seniority obligation.** On a small team, the senior person's output includes the people they level up, not just their own commits.
- **Explaining fundamentals sharpens your own understanding.** Teaching React basics forced me to articulate things I had been doing on intuition.

---

## Interview Usage

Good for:

- Tell me about mentoring or coaching someone
- How do you help junior engineers grow?
- Tell me about a time you invested in a teammate
- What does seniority mean to you?

**Suggested framing:** Concrete and modest. The success belongs to her (she did the learning); my contribution was showing up consistently and teaching fundamentals instead of shortcuts.
